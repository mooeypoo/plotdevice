Plot Device keeps three things separate: the **interface's language**, the **book's language**, and the **direction of each paragraph**.

<Shot name="bilingual" alt="A Hebrew chapter, right to left, in the English interface" />

## The book's language

You choose it when you start a book. It sets the direction paragraphs run by default: a Hebrew book runs right to left, an English one left to right. The few words on the page itself follow it too: the chapter's number above its title, the hint in an empty chapter, and "Untitled poem".

## A paragraph or a phrase the other way

When a paragraph, or a phrase inside one, should run the other way, select it and use **<Ui k="toolbar-format" />** or the bubble's **⋯**:

- **<Ui k="direction-paragraph-ltr" />** and **<Ui k="direction-paragraph-rtl" />** set the whole paragraph. **<Ui k="direction-paragraph-clear" />** returns it to the book's direction.
- **<Ui k="direction-phrase-ltr" />** and **<Ui k="direction-phrase-rtl" />** set just the selected words, such as an English name inside a Hebrew sentence, so its punctuation lands in the right place. **<Ui k="direction-phrase-clear" />** removes the setting.

Plot Device saves a direction only where it's needed, in a way other apps keep, so nothing is lost when the book is opened elsewhere. For how it's written, see [Chapters and the contents](/docs/technical/files/chapters/#direction) in the technical docs.

## Niqqud

To add vowel marks to Hebrew, put the cursor just after a letter and choose **<Ui k="toolbar-format" /> → <Ui k="niqqud-menu" />**, or press **Ctrl+Shift+N** (⌘⇧N on a Mac). Choose a mark to add it, and again to take it off; the arrow keys move from letter to letter. It works in every chapter and page. See [Niqqud](/docs/writing/poems/#niqqud) in Writing poems.

Niqqud is kept exactly as you type it, and searching ignores it: יעל finds יַעֵל.

## The interface's language

**☰ → <Ui k="settings-open" /> → <Ui k="settings-language" />** changes the language of menus and buttons. It doesn't change your book. Write a Hebrew book in an English interface, or the other way round.

## Which words follow which language

- **The interface's language** is for everything around the page: menus, buttons, questions, and the sidebar's **+ New…** buttons. In a Hebrew poetry collection with Plot Device in English, the sidebar says **+ New poem** and **+ New section**.
- **The book's language** is for the page itself: the text and its direction, the chapter's number above its title, the hint in an empty chapter, and "Untitled poem". In that same collection, the page says „שיר 1”.
- **Names you choose yourself**, such as a kind of page you call "Factions" or sections you call "Cycles", are shown as you typed them, whatever the language. Plot Device has nothing to translate them from.
